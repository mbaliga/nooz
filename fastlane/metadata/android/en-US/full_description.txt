A news reader built around a different question: not "what happened," but
"what did I miss." It shows the shape of everything that came in from the
sources you chose, laid alongside the shape of what you actually opened and
read — so the gap between the two is something you can actually see.

It never claims to show "all the news." Every total in this app names its own
denominator: coverage, topic mix, and every comparison are always drawn from
the sources you chose to add, not from some universal feed.

What it refuses to do:
• No telemetry, analytics, or crash reporting. Nothing leaves your device
  except your own requests to the sources you add.
• No push notifications. Nothing interrupts you.
• No engagement-driven ranking by default — chronological or grouped by
  source; any other order is one you chose, and it's labeled.
• Feeds end. An explicit marker shows where a source's feed stops, never an
  infinite scroll built to keep you reading.
• No streaks, badges, or gamification of any kind.
• Every classification, flag, or number is tappable to show the exact rule,
  lexicon entry, or formula that produced it — nothing is a black box.

It also includes an optional, on-device tool for spotting loaded language in
what you read (a "slammed," a "so-called," an unexplained intensifier) —
flagged with its exact reason, and only ever changed with your explicit,
reversible say-so.

Add feeds by URL, browse a short list of pre-checked starter sources, or
import an OPML file. Read comfortably, with full-text extraction when a feed
only gives you a summary. Nothing about how you read is ranked, pushed, or
gamified back at you.
