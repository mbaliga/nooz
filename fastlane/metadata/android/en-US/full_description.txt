A news reader built around a different question: not "what happened," but
"what did I miss." It shows the shape of everything that came in from the
sources you chose, laid alongside the shape of what you actually opened and
read, so the gap between the two is something you can see.

It never claims to show "all the news." Every total in this app names its own
denominator: coverage, topic mix, and every comparison are always drawn from
the sources you chose to add, not from some universal feed.

WHAT IT REFUSES TO DO
• No telemetry, analytics or crash reporting. Nothing leaves your device except
  your own requests to the sources you add.
• No push notifications. Nothing interrupts you.
• No engagement-driven ranking by default. Chronological or grouped by source;
  any other order is one you chose, and it is labeled.
• Feeds end. An explicit marker shows where a source's feed stops, never an
  infinite scroll built to keep you reading.
• No streaks, badges or gamification of any kind.
• Every classification, flag or number is tappable to show the exact rule,
  lexicon entry or formula that produced it. Nothing is a black box.

READING
• Add feeds by URL, browse a short list of pre-checked starter sources, or
  import an OPML file.
• Full-text extraction when a feed only gives you a summary.
• Light paper theme by default, with system and dark options.
• Nothing about how you read is ranked, pushed or gamified back at you.

SPOTTING LOADED LANGUAGE
An optional, on-device tool for noticing loaded language in what you read: a
"slammed," a "so-called," an unexplained intensifier. Each flag shows its exact
reason, and only ever changes anything with your explicit, reversible say-so.

Everything runs on your device. There is no account, and there is no server of
ours for there to be an account on.
